Cases and permissions. A graph-only screen of the eight previously observed development cases admitted c03 and c06. Each allowed two successive tie closures with a load gain at each closure in at least one order. All legal prefixes were retained, including a first closure with zero immediate gain. Both planners could close healthy ties while preserving radiality and continuous service to energized loads. Sectionalizing switches and load-readiness controls were fixed. All closures were consecutive at one-minute intervals; all subsequent slots were waits. Each case admitted five full schedules: all-wait, either of the two permissible ties on its own, or both ties in either order.

Published rule and adaptations. The reimplementation advances one stage per closure. Its priorities are maximum restored terminal load, fewer operations, higher minimum energized-phase voltage and lower real losses. It keeps the selected predecessor for each tie set to reconstruct an action sequence. Voltages and losses used for state ranking come from the sample immediately after the most recent switching action. The common evaluation uses normal active load instead of the published terminal apparent load. A separate calculation confirmed identical primary rankings and ties for all five prefixes in each case under both measures. Action identifiers resolved exact ties deterministically. The common three-phase verifier applies its stated voltage, thermal and model-consistency checks. The published phase-angle limits are outside this comparison. The accepted sequence is evaluated from its own load pickup history, including its waiting tail. The restricted IGRS generates the complete small set of legal tie-closure sequences in place of its standard staged-load generator. The full published method also allows paired switching operations outside the present action set.

Cost and outcomes. Each arm started with empty caches and paid for its own width-eight Beam fallback. Within one task the two stages could reuse identical physical inputs. Each stage had a budget of 256 logical points; each cold task had a total budget of 512 physical evaluations and 240 s. The Viterbi state selection used its own paid electrical information and did not use the Beam incumbent for ranking. Each of the two schedules it selected passed the initial check and all 90 prescribed samples. Both matched the corresponding Beam plan exactly, so the final merge retained that plan on the energy tie. The restricted IGRS eliminated all four nonempty additional candidates on the basis of certified energy. Each Viterbi task evaluated four additional distinct electrical inputs. The reported external cost includes its own fallback. An unprotected standalone Viterbi runtime was not measured. These two single-run comparisons concern the specified common subproblem and previously observed fault–event cases.
